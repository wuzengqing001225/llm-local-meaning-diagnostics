\title{When Local Definitions Matter: Diagnosing Source-Linked Language Model Errors}
\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
An answer can agree with a retrieved passage yet be wrong under a definition elsewhere in the document. Such errors are hard to catch from the passage or the reader's confidence alone. We study source-linked diagnostic questions about locally defined terms and ask what a corpus owner can learn \emph{before} calling a deployed reader. On matched synthetic and contract questions, a 7B model's low support for the source-implied answer ranks stronger readers' errors with AUROC 0.809--0.848. In a scored CUAD frame, the highest-risk 23\% of questions contain 66\% of estimated reader errors, providing a practical order for source review. The score also predicts errors where local and usual meanings agree, so it is an error-risk index rather than a detector of meaning conflict. On synthetic questions, supplying the source definition raises accuracy from 0.699 to 0.966; a gloss elicited from the reader's prior reaches 0.708. Confidence and sample-agreement signals discriminate poorly on redefined common words. A selected 27-question source review finds substantial item-validity problems, and definition selection on new questions has not been shown to beat frequency. The resulting contribution is an offline diagnostic: document owners can test where answers depend on local definitions, preserve those definitions with their scope, and prioritise review without claiming a validated policy for arbitrary future requests.
\end{abstract}
