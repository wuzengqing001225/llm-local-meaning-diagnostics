\section{Errors under missing definitions can be stable}
\label{sec:stable}
\input{section/table_prediction.tex}
Table~\ref{tab:prediction} shows errors on source-linked diagnostic questions. Without the definition, DeepSeek V4 Flash errs on 28.5\% of matched synthetic questions and GPT-6-astra on 18.2\%. The design-weighted estimate is 13.2\% in the 6,529-question CUAD scoring frame; this is a rate for retained diagnostic questions, not all contract uses or live requests. Adding a definition lowers error in every tested row, but a corrected answer alone does not establish that the original error was specifically a conflict with ordinary meaning. It does show why a system must treat local definitions as answer-bearing source material.

The designed synthetic classes help isolate where local meaning has the largest effect. DeepSeek V4 Flash errs on 44.2\% of questions about familiar words given a conflicting meaning, against 25.3\% for coined words and 14.8\% for words retaining their usual meaning. GPT-6-astra shows the same ordering (36.5\%, 12.7\%, and 4.2\%; Figure~\ref{fig:phenomenon}a). Table~\ref{tab:examples} gives contract instances where an ordinary reading would broaden the local scope. The class comparison is consistent with a misleading prior, but it does not label the cause of every individual error.

Many errors are stable enough to survive a common runtime safeguard. On 761 synthetic questions, DeepSeek V4 Flash gave a greedy answer and ten sampled answers. Among 229 greedy errors, 93 (40.6\%) repeat the same wrong answer in all ten samples, and the sampled majority remains wrong for 142 (62\%). Majority voting would correct some errors, but 65\% of errors on redefined common words survive, versus 43\% on usual-meaning terms. Agreement across samples \citep{self-consistency} is therefore not a reliable signal that a local definition was unnecessary.

The tested readers also share many difficult items. On questions answered by all readers in each corpus, 78\% of GPT-6-astra's synthetic errors overlap DeepSeek V4 Flash errors, 70\% of its Reddit errors overlap DeepSeek V4.1 Flash errors, and 94\% of its DeFi errors overlap at least one of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GPT-5.6-terra. Under independent error sets, the corresponding expected shares are 29\%, 30\%, and 36\% (Figure~\ref{fig:phenomenon}b). These cross-model overlaps make a reusable corpus audit plausible, while the heterogeneous reader families and question sets do not establish that a risk index will retain its ranking for every future model.
