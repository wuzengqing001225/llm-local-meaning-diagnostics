\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Hallucination, knowledge conflict, and context faithfulness.}
Hallucination research asks whether an answer agrees with world knowledge or with the supplied input \citep{space}. Knowledge-conflict benchmarks place evidence in the context that contradicts parametric knowledge and measure which source the model follows \citep{longpre-etal-2021-entity,conflictbank,knowledge-conflict-faithfulness,kc-survey}. Context-aware and adaptive contrastive decoding, together with steering methods, push the model toward the context \citep{cad,adacad,coiecd,contextfocus,taming-kc}. Redefinition tasks state a new meaning and test whether the model adopts it \citep{inverse-scaling,redefine-pitfalls}. All of these assume that the needed information is in the input or that the error contradicts it. Silent misreadings begin when the information is absent and the answer contradicts nothing the model can see. Contrastive decoding, which reweights information already in the input, cannot supply it. With Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct as a white-box reader, it lowers accuracy by 2.8 points $[-4.4, -1.0]$ on 1,200 CUAD questions when only retrieved passages are available (Appendix~\ref{app:interventions}). This work addresses the step before faithfulness, recognizing what a reader must be told.

\paragraph{Local language and word meaning.}
Studies of community language, semantic change, new concepts, and ordinary meaning in law describe how word senses vary and whether models know them \citep{lucy-bamman-glossaries,lsc-survey,semeval2020-lsc,slang,legal-interpretation}. We measure how models behave on individual occurrences when a local meaning is missing. A term-level measure of how far the local meaning is from the usual one separates designed term classes but predicts errors weakly on real corpora (Appendix~\ref{app:meaning-difference}), because whether an occurrence is misread depends on what the question asks.

\paragraph{Error detection and question difficulty.}
Sampling consistency, semantic entropy, and verbalized confidence detect many hallucinations \citep{selfcheckgpt,semantic-uncertainty,semantic-entropy,verbal-confidence}. We identify a class of errors they miss. Item response theory and answer-plausibility methods estimate question difficulty across models \citep{vania-etal-2021-comparing,byrd-srivastava-2022-predicting,mozafari-etal-2026-question}. Our small-model score belongs to this family. It estimates the difficulty of diagnostic items tied to a source occurrence and a local definition, for building and auditing evaluations. SlimPLM uses a small model's answer to an incoming question to decide what a larger model should retrieve \citep{tan-etal-2024-small}, which complements retrieval and long-context use \citep{rag,lost-middle}.
