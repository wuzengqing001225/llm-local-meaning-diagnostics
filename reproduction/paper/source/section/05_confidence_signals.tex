\section{Confidence alone weakly identifies the risky cases}
\label{sec:detect}
The errors above raise a practical question: can a reader signal that it needs a definition? On the 456 synthetic questions shared with the proxy, disagreement among ten sampled answers, option-vote entropy, and negative stated confidence \citep{selfcheckgpt,semantic-uncertainty,verbal-confidence} each rank DeepSeek V4 Flash errors at about 0.616 AUROC (Figure~\ref{fig:silent}a). On redefined common words the values are 0.557, 0.557, and 0.463, weaker than on coined and usual-meaning terms (Appendix~\ref{app:signals-by-class}). Our option-vote entropy is narrower than semantic entropy over free-form answers \citep{semantic-entropy}; the result concerns these implemented signals, not every uncertainty method. The proxy's key-free uncertainty likewise reaches only 0.537 and 0.523 on redefined common words for the two readers. A low-uncertainty answer should therefore not by itself be treated as evidence that the corpus supplied all relevant definitions.

Jev \citep{jev} offers a separate test using a decision model with native confidence. Without a definition, that confidence ranks its own errors at 0.643 on synthetic, 0.660 on Reddit, and 0.636 on CUAD questions; with a model-drafted local gloss the values rise to 0.815, 0.790, and 0.868 (Figure~\ref{fig:silent}b). The comparison changes both the available information and the error set, so it does not isolate calibration as a cause. It does show that this confidence channel gives little guidance about errors when local content is missing, precisely when a retrieval system needs to decide what to supply.
