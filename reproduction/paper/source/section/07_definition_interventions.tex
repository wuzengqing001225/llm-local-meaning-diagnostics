\section{Source definitions repair errors that the model's own gloss does not}
\label{sec:repair}
\paragraph{Models follow a definition when told.}
On 1,244 human-written redefinition items of the Inverse Scaling task \citep{inverse-scaling}, GPT-5.6-terra and GPT-6-astra answer by the stated meaning on 95.0\% and 99.8\% of items, and with the redefinition removed both answer by the usual meaning on about 98\%. These items involve no model-drafted keys. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash follows the stated meaning on only 63.0\%, so disregarding a stated definition is a separate failure whose rate differs sharply between readers. For the two GPT readers, nearly all of the failure lies in not having been told.

\paragraph{The repair comes from the source, not from the word.}
On 761 synthetic questions whose drafter saw usage sentences but no definition, DeepSeek V4 Flash accuracy is 0.699 without a note, 0.966 with the source definition, 0.949 with a model-drafted gloss written from that source, and 0.708 with the reader's own gloss of the term written under a domain prompt (Figure~\ref{fig:silent}c). The source definition repairs 209 errors and harms 6 correct answers, while the reader's own gloss repairs 86 and harms 79. On the 180 questions where no option is singled out by word overlap with any of the three texts, accuracy rises from 0.617 to 0.911 with the source definition and falls to 0.544 with the reader's gloss (Appendix~\ref{app:lexical-cues}). Asking a model to state its understanding of a term before answering restates its prior and does not help.

\paragraph{The wording of a definition sets both repairs and harms.}
On the exploratory corpora the community's own entry raises accuracy from 0.657 to 0.932 on Reddit glossary questions and from 0.672 to 0.940 on DeFi, with 243 and 70 repairs against 9 and 3 harms. A model-drafted gloss of the same entries repairs 171 and 42 but harms 58 and 20, and the reader's own gloss harms more answers than it repairs on Reddit (117 against 82) (Appendix~\ref{app:interventions}). A loosely scoped note can turn a correct answer into a wrong one, so automatically generated glossaries need to be tested before use. The appendix shows that $\Delta p$ ranks which errors a given wording repairs (Appendix~\ref{app:statistics}).

\paragraph{In contracts, a definition is not sufficient.}
Extracted contract definitions repair 163 errors and harm 83 correct answers, raising accuracy from 0.787 to 0.840 (Figure~\ref{fig:strata}b). Of 320 original errors, 157 remain wrong. Contract definitions often refer to schedules or other clauses, so a retrieval system has to supply the linked material as well as the definition itself. Choosing which definitions to write first by term frequency is a reasonable default, since no score-based choice outperformed it in small Reddit and CUAD comparisons (Appendix~\ref{app:new}).

\paragraph{Public benchmarks rarely test these boundaries.}
On public human-written tasks, adding definitions changes scores little. CUAD clause extraction changes by at most 0.3 F1 points and ContractNLI \citep{contractnli} accuracy by at most 1.1 points (Appendix~\ref{app:boundaries}). Both tasks ask what a clause says rather than whether a particular case falls inside a defined term, so few of their items turn on a definition's boundary. The same contracts produce errors on 13.2\% of the diagnostic questions that do. Evaluating systems that read local text requires questions built to reach these boundaries, which the released sets provide.
